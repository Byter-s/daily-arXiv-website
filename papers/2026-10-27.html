[1] Rethinking Structured Generation: Can Graph-Based Reasoning Resolve Ambiguity? cs.CL | cs.AIPDF

Ratun Rahman, Atit Pokharel

TL;DR: 论文重新审视结构化生成中“输入到单一结构化输出”的确定性假设，指出自然语言输入常存在多种语义有效但结构不同的解释。作者将任务重构为歧义感知推理问题，采用保留多有效答案的多有效学习框架，并将候选结构化输出表示为图，通过语义对齐和关系一致性进行图推理选择。

Details

Motivation: 现有结构化生成方法依赖单参考监督，把输入中的歧义压缩为单一预测，导致在欠指定输入下输出不稳定、不一致，并抑制其他合法解释。

Result: 在结构化生成基准上，该方法在准确性、稳定性和不同歧义程度下的鲁棒性方面均取得一致提升；摘要未给出具体数值或SOTA声明。

Insight: 创新点在于显式建模结构化生成中的歧义，从单输出预测转向集合级推理；用图表示候选输出并基于语义对齐与关系一致性选择，相比token级似然比较更具原则性。

Abstract: Structured generation is typically formulated as a deterministic mapping from input to a single structured output. This assumption is fundamentally misaligned with natural language, which often admits multiple structurally distinct yet semantically valid interpretations. As a result, existing methods rely on single-reference supervision that collapses ambiguity into one prediction, leading to unstable and inconsistent outputs under underspecified inputs. We reformulate structured generation as an \textit{ambiguity-aware inference problem}, where the objective is to reason over a set of plausible structured outputs rather than predict a single solution. We show that standard training inherently suppresses valid alternatives, and instead adopt a multi-valid learning framework that preserves ambiguity during optimization. To operationalize this formulation, we construct a candidate space of structured outputs, represent each candidate as a graph capturing entities and their relationships, and perform selection via graph-based reasoning over semantic alignment and relational consistency. This enables principled comparison across competing interpretations beyond token-level likelihoods. Experiments on structured generation benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements in accuracy, stability, and robustness under varying levels of ambiguity. These results highlight the importance of modeling ambiguity explicitly and suggest a shift from single-output prediction to set-level reasoning in structured generation.

[4] Free Lunch for LLMs Confidence: The RL Critic Already Knows cs.CLPDF

TL;DR: 论文发现LLM的置信度估计与强化学习中的价值函数（critic）训练在数学上等价，因此对于使用基于价值的RL训练的LLM，可以直接复用已有的critic作为置信度估计器，无需额外训练或数据。该复用方法在多个领域上取得了最佳置信度估计和校准性能，且不影响任务表现。

Details

Motivation: 传统LLM置信度估计需要专门的训练阶段，消耗额外数据和计算资源。论文旨在探究置信度训练的本质，寻找一种无需额外成本即可获得可靠置信度估计的方法。

Result: 在数学、代码、工具调用和通用推理等十几个领域上，复用的critic在每个分布内分割上均为最佳置信度估计器，在16个设置中有14个达到最佳校准，同时保持任务性能不变。

Insight: 理论贡献在于证明基于正确性结果的置信度估计与critic训练等价，从而将RL中的critic直接转化为现成的置信度估计器；这一发现为LLM置信度估计提供了无需额外训练的创新途径，并可提供生成过程中间步骤的置信度。

Abstract: Reliable confidence estimation is essential for trustworthy deployment of large language models (LLMs), but dedicated confidence training stage requires additional data and compute. We ask a fundamental question: what prediction problem does confidence training actually solve? Inspired by prior work on external confidence estimation, we compare self- and external estimators of comparable capacity trained on the same (question, response, correctness) triples. We show that an external model trained solely on a policy’s behavioral traces can estimate the policy’s confidence as accurately as the policy itself, without accessing the policy’s internal representations. This points to a deeper connection with value function learning in reinforcement learning (RL): under a correctness-based outcome, we prove that confidence estimation and critic training are equivalent, both predicting the likelihood that a policy’s response will be correct from its trajectory. A simple consequence follows: for LLMs trained with value-based RL, the existing critic can be reused as an off-the-shelf confidence estimator with no additional training or data. The policy remains unchanged, so its task performance is preserved, and the critic provides confidence throughout generation, including intermediate reasoning steps. Across math, code, tool calling, and general reasoning spanning a dozen domains, the reused critic is the best confidence estimator on every in-distribution split and the best-calibrated on 14 of 16, without hurting the task performance.

In separate single-turn calls, models are asked for $P(X)$, for $P(z)$ over the latent’s states, and for $P(X|z)$; a causal family applies the same test to $P(y|do(x))$ and the back-door adjustment. Across seven models, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 are the most coherent (mean absolute residual 0.08 and 0.12 log-odds, about 2 percentage points). Claude Haiku 5.5, Gemini 3.8 Flash and DeepSeek V4-Pro show persistent incoherence (0.27-0.35 log-odds) that is mostly item-specific rather than a shared offset. Within every model, larger residuals go with larger errors against the truth (Spearman 0.40-0.62), so the residual, which needs no ground truth, flags unreliable answers. Reasoning length does not predict coherence, across models or within a model across rewordings of the same question. Simple consistency checks pass almost everywhere; the failures are specific to reasoning through a latent variable. Code and data: https://github.com/ppotash/latent-coherence
